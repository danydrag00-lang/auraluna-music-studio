# Research-first music quality standard

Sources:
- Scarratt et al., sleep playlist audio features: https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0278813 (225,626 tracks).
- Sleep vs relaxation playlists: https://doi.org/10.1177/10298649241269011
- 2025 narrative review: https://doi.org/10.3389/frsle.2025.1707162
- Ambient production: https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/sound-design-ambient-music

These are general research and production findings, not measured benchmarks of specific commercial tracks.
## Current engine gap
The engine schedules recorded isolated notes, has fixed four-chord cycles, short early reflections, simple amplitude control, and no measured loudness or listening panel. Slow tempo alone does not ensure soothing music.
## Generation workflow
1. Select a mood-specific production brief: sleep, spa, zen, Indian meditation, cinematic or focus.
2. Compare licensed reference tracks using actual audio if available; derive broad descriptors, not copied melodies or recordings.
3. Plan a coherent harmonic palette, foreground instrument, supporting textures and phrases.
4. Render three distinct short candidates from licensed local assets.
5. Measure peak, RMS, short-window level variation, spectral brightness, clipping and silence. These are diagnostic proxies, not a relaxation score.
6. Audition at matched playback levels. Reject harsh attacks, mechanical phrasing, obvious looping and competing voices.
7. Extend only the approved candidate, check transitions and preserve licence evidence.
## Acceptance
No missing assets, clipping, invalid audio or undocumented source rights. Human listening is required for musical quality acceptance. Do not label a track professionally mastered on the basis of automated measurements alone.
## Next investments
Soft velocity multisamples, recorded phrases with documented keys, authentic sustained textures, measured loudness and reference comparison, natural decay and longer spatial tails. No artificial noise substitution.
